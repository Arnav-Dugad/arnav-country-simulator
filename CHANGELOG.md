# Changelog

The release workflow publishes the section that matches the tag as the GitHub Release notes.

## [0.66.0] - 2026-11-08

- **Tourism & soft power**, a new page from the World tab with new AI-painted art
- **Tourism** starts from real data and grows with stability, peace, heritage sites, culture builds and your reputation. Extra visitors bring growth and jobs
- **World heritage:** nominate old towns, national parks, ancient ruins, coasts and reefs, sacred sites or modern landmarks. A committee decides 18 months later
- **Host world events:** bid for a World Expo, a Football World Cup or the Summer Games. Win the vote and you spend years preparing, then enjoy a year in the spotlight with fireworks, pride and tourists
- **Soft power:** a 0–100 score and a world rank. A higher score slowly warms every country toward you, while wars, media crackdowns and exposed spies cost you

- **Spies & intelligence.** An Intelligence section on the World page, with a new AI-painted operations room
  - Set your agency's budget (Small to Massive). A bigger agency is stronger, runs more operations at once and catches more foreign spies
- **Covert operations** from any country's page: gather intelligence (an edge in any war with them), steal technology (research points), sabotage, meddle in their politics, or arm their rebels
  - Each operation shows its odds of success and of exposure. Exposed operations mean expelled diplomats, angry neighbours and bad headlines at home
- **Hostile countries spy on you too:** spy rings caught, hackers stealing research, sabotage at refineries and banks

- **Wealth & state firms**, a new page from the Economy tab
- **A national stock market:** an index (100 when you take office) that rises with growth and stability, falls when you raise interest rates, and follows world markets. Booms make people feel richer; crashes make the news
- **A sovereign wealth fund:** deposit or withdraw money, choose the mix of safe bonds and global stocks, and decide whether its returns go to the budget. Big oil exporters start with a fund
- **State firms:** the national oil company, power utility, railways, post, airline, telecoms, state bank, steel, mining and defence makers. Who owns what at the start follows how open your country is
  - Privatise a firm for cash and long-run growth, at the cost of layoffs and anger
  - Buy one out, or seize it for free and watch investors flee
  - Bring in professional managers to make a firm more efficient and raise its dividends

## [0.63.0] - 2026-11-08

- **Media & propaganda.** A new section on the Laws page, with an AI-painted press room. Choose how free your press is: a free press, guided coverage or state-run media
  - More control means friendlier coverage and calmer streets, but unhappier people, dirtier government, wary investors and colder democracies
  - Tightening control costs political capital and sparks protests, loud ones in a free country. Credibility is capped lower under a controlled press
- **State media campaigns:** six months on every screen. Choose Proud nation, Healthy lives, Calm and order, Green habits, Invest in us, Behind our troops (wartime only) or Discredit the opposition
  - Their strength depends on how far state media reaches and how much people trust you, and repeating a message wears it thin
  - A smear campaign can leak, and a free press is far more likely to dig it up

- **A redesigned Build tab.** It opens on a dashboard with a new AI-painted header, then shows eight category cards with their own artwork and coverage bars (Transport, Energy, Health & care, Education, Housing & cities, Economy, Culture & sport, Defence). Each category page lists every build with how many you have, the price of one, and a bar showing coverage now and once the work under way is finished
- **Best value now:** the three civilian builds that would do the most for your country per dollar right now, one tap away
- **Builders have a capacity.** Your construction sector can take on only so much work a year (more with a bigger Infrastructure budget). Go over it and every site slows down, and the building boom pushes up inflation
- **Rush orders:** pay extra to finish a batch twice as fast, from the new Under construction list or the order card
- **Condition:** roads, hospitals and schools wear out when the Infrastructure budget is cut, and deliver less until it is restored
- **A clearer order card:** a coverage curve shows the diminishing returns of each extra unit, plus how busy your builders will be
- **Approval is always in the top bar**, as a coloured ring. Tap it to see what drives it

## [0.61.0] - 2026-11-08

- **Research and the space race.** A new Research tab on the Build page with 25 technologies in six branches (Economy, Energy, Health, Transport, Space, Military). Research points come from your research budget, universities and labs, and the size of your economy. Each breakthrough lasts forever: AI, quantum computing, fusion power, longevity therapies, self-driving vehicles, drone swarms, hypersonic missiles and more

## [0.58.0] - 2026-11-08

- **War.** You can now declare war on a neighbour (or, with a strong enough military, on a coastal country overseas) from its country page, and hostile, much stronger neighbours may attack you
  - Each month brings a battle on the front, decided by both armies' strength, morale, allies and a defender's edge. Winning takes ground; losing gives it up. Occupied land is striped on the map in the occupier's colour, and a pulsing front line with battle flashes joins the capitals
  - **War room** (from the country or the World page): ground held, battle odds, morale, casualties and recent battles. Mobilize the reserves, call on your allies, or offer peace
  - **Peace terms:** keep the land you hold (its people and part of its economy join yours), demand reparations, or return to the old borders. When a war goes badly, the enemy may force terms on you, and losing most of your land ends your rule
  - Wars cost money every month, slow the economy and push prices up; people rally at first and grow weary later. Attacking angers the world; being attacked wins sympathy
- New AI-generated war-room scene; new achievements: Victor and Peacemaker

## [0.57.0] - 2026-11-08

## [0.48.0] - 2026-11-08

- **Realistic is properly harder now:** voters are tougher and crises bite harder (the balance report showed it had become almost as forgiving as Standard)
- Your avatar in the main-menu Settings shows your last country's flag instead of a question mark
- Calmer Inbox: foreign leaders ask for disaster aid at most once a year
- **Inequality.** Every country starts at its real Gini index (World Bank). It shifts slowly with welfare spending, income tax, joblessness and laws (minimum wage, wealth tax, basic income narrow the gap; privatisation widens it). A growing gap costs happiness and stability; a shrinking one wins both
- **Ageing.** Each country starts at its real share of people over 65, which rises every year (fastest in rich countries with few births, slower where young migrants arrive). Every extra point adds pension and care costs and shrinks the workforce. Raising the pension age softens both. The Budget page shows what ageing costs you
- **Corruption.** A clean-government score (0–100) starts from each country's real World Bank rating. Anti-corruption laws, press freedom and stability raise it; cleaner government means faster growth, cheaper projects and fewer scandals. Once every two years you can crack down on corruption from the Laws page: officials push back at first, then it pays off for years
- **Central bank independence matters.** Taking control of interest rates yourself now worries markets (higher borrowing costs) and, if you keep rates too low, makes inflation expectations climb faster. The governor may even threaten to resign: give the bank its independence back, fire them, or promise to raise rates
- **Trade wars.** Put tariffs on any country from its page: your factories get shelter and the treasury collects duties, but prices rise. Big, proud or unfriendly countries often hit back, slowing your growth and angering rural voters, whose exports suffer first. Tariffs show on the map as red arcs
- Law cards show their effect on inequality, country pages show each country's Gini, very unequal countries get a "Fairer society" goal, and your advisors warn when the gap grows
